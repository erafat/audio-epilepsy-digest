Learning Objectives

  1. Identify the denominator and endpoint in the Medicaid coprescription study.
  2. Interpret documented counseling differences in a selected records review.
  3. Read survey information and timing measures with their distinct denominators.

Questions

1. In Harrison’s Medicaid study, 7,154 people had at least 14 days of enzyme-inducing ASM overlap with a susceptible contraceptive method. What is the correct denominator and interpretation?
Correct answer: B. The denominator was 21,923 people with an included contraceptive fill or implant-placement procedure; 7,154 (32.6%) had claims-derived overlap with an enzyme-inducing antiseizure medication. Claims did not measure actual use, counseling, pregnancy, or contraceptive failure. Harrison, Results and Methods.
2. Tao’s records review found less documentation of a pre-pregnancy teratogenicity discussion in the pediatric-transition group than the adult-established group. What can be concluded?
Correct answer: C. The 2025 single-center review compared 21 pediatric-transition with 152 adult-established patients and measured documentation. It did not test a causal care effect or pregnancy outcome. Tao, Methods and Discussion.
3. In Baker’s online survey, what does the roughly 53% result describe?
Correct answer: B. Baker reports 446 of 847 responses to the practitioner-provided contraception-information item. The self-selected survey does not establish population prevalence or contraceptive failure. Baker, Results section 3.4.
4. What is the safest way to put the Harrison, Tao, and Baker findings together?
Correct answer: C. Harrison measured claims-derived prescription overlap, Tao reviewed records before a first delivered pregnancy, and Baker asked survey respondents about information and timing. Their groups and endpoints differ, so the episode offers a careful clinic question rather than a causal chain. Harrison, Tao, and Baker, Methods and Results.
